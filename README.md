# Viggo's Studio

Create a minimalist one-page website for an artist and songwriter named **Viggo**.

The visual direction should feel like an evolution of a personal artist bio sheet: understated, editorial, intimate, slightly unconventional, and fashion-forward.

Think:

* minimalist musician/artist website
* early-2000s editorial design
* understated European art direction
* slightly raw, personal, and cool rather than polished/corporate
* inspired by the restraint and simplicity of artists like Lorde, without copying any specific existing website

### Overall visual style

Use:

* a soft gray, warm off-white, stone, or muted neutral background
* black or very dark gray typography
* mostly lowercase typography
* lots of negative space
* simple sans-serif type
* restrained typography hierarchy rather than oversized marketing headlines
* asymmetrical or editorial spacing where appropriate
* subtle hover effects only
* no gradients
* no bright colors
* no flashy animations
* no glossy music-industry aesthetic
* no generic “rock star” or “Spotify artist” design

The page should feel like someone designed a beautiful digital artist bio rather than a conventional commercial website.

Keep it highly readable and mobile-friendly.

### Hero

At the top, display:

**viggo**

and underneath in smaller type:

**artist / songwriter**

Do not use a giant full-screen hero banner.

Leave space for one candid editorial portrait. The image should feel naturally integrated into the layout rather than like a corporate headshot.

### About

Use a text-forward layout with a short first-person bio.

The tone should feel conversational, intelligent, self-aware, warm, and slightly playful rather than formal.

Use placeholder text for now, approximately 150–200 words.

The final bio will discuss:

* being a New York-based artist and songwriter
* songwriting as a way of making sense of the world
* writing about love, loss, identity, relationships, and everyday life
* experience working internationally with artists
* an interest in emotionally driven pop, melody, atmosphere, European music, and Eurovision
* writing both for himself and for other artists

Keep the paragraph width relatively narrow so it reads like an editorial column.

### Selected Experience

Create a minimalist section titled:

**selected experience**

Present the information as a clean typographic list rather than résumé cards.

**north macedonia**
internal selection 2017
internal selection 2018 — 2nd place

**czechia**
internal selection 2025 — 2nd place

**luxembourg**
luxembourg song contest 2026

**ukraine**
english-language consultant for artists and labels
2020–2022

Use understated spacing and typography. Avoid icons, progress bars, badges, logos, flags, or decorative graphics.

### Upcoming

Create a simple section titled:

**upcoming**

Feature:

**debut ep · 2027**

with a short line beneath:

“I’m currently working on my debut EP, planned for release in 2027.”

This section should feel intriguing and understated rather than promotional.

### Contact

Create a simple but visually distinct contact section near the bottom.

Use:

**say hello**

For songwriting, artist collaborations, and creative inquiries:

**[hello@viggomusic.com](mailto:hello@viggomusic.com)**

Make the email clickable.

Avoid a complicated contact form unless absolutely necessary.

### Footer

Keep the footer extremely minimal:

**© viggo**

### Important

Do not add:

* music players
* Spotify links
* a discography section
* fake releases
* testimonials
* fake press quotes
* statistics
* social media icons unless added manually later
* client logos
* “Listen Now” buttons
* “Book Me” buttons
* generic calls to action
* invented information

The site should feel confident enough that it does not need to oversell itself.

The overall impression should be:
**personal, artistic, understated, intelligent, contemporary, slightly mysterious, and effortlessly cool.**
